01 · Learn

Obesity is more complex than one number.

Obesity is a complex chronic health condition associated with excess body fat and potential health risks. It is influenced by multiple interacting factors. A person's body size alone does not tell their whole health story.

🧬

Biology & family history

Genetics, hormones, health conditions, and some medications can influence body weight.

🥗

Daily routines

Eating patterns, movement, sleep, and stress can all play a role in health.

🏙️

Environment & access

Time, finances, food availability, safe places to move, and access to care shape people's options.

💚

Health is more than weight

Well-being includes physical health, mental health, sleep, energy, and access to appropriate care.

🩺

Personalized care matters

Health needs differ. Qualified health professionals can help people understand personal risks and options.

🫶

Respect matters

Stigma and blame do not help people access care. Everyone deserves respectful support.

Keep in mind: This guide provides general education, not an individual diagnosis or treatment plan. Talk with a qualified healthcare professional about personal health concerns.
02 · Myth or fact?

Challenge what you think you know.

Tap a statement to reveal a quick explanation. This is a self-guided learning activity—not a test of your personal health.

Understanding the full picture can replace oversimplified assumptions with better information and more compassionate conversations.

Myth. Body weight is affected by many factors, including biology, genetics, medications, sleep, stress, and environment. Blaming individuals oversimplifies a complex health issue.
Fact. Sleep is one of several factors associated with weight and health. Many adults ages 18–64 are advised to aim for 7–9 hours per night, although individual needs vary.
Myth. Some changes can be simple, such as planning a few meals, carrying a refillable water bottle, taking movement breaks, or looking into available food resources. Not every option is accessible to every student.
Fact. Body size alone cannot establish an individual's health status. A qualified health professional can assess personal concerns in context.
Myth. Health, culture, finances, food access, disability, schedules, and preferences differ. A sustainable approach should fit an individual's circumstances.
03 · Everyday wellness

Small steps for real college life.

You do not need to change everything at once. Pick ideas that are realistic for your schedule, budget, needs, and preferences.

No perfection required. These are options to explore—not rules to judge yourself by. Adapt them to your abilities and circumstances.
  1. 1
    Make meals easierPlan a few meals or snacks ahead, use a grocery list, and consider affordable foods that fit your needs.
  2. 2
    Build balanced mealsWhen practical, combine foods such as fruits or vegetables, whole grains, and protein sources.
  3. 3
    Add movement that works for youWalk between classes, stretch during study breaks, or explore an activity you enjoy.
  4. 4
    Protect your sleepConsider a consistent sleep and wake time and a wind-down routine during demanding weeks.
  5. 5
    Care for your mental well-beingTake breaks, connect with supportive people, and seek professional help when needed.
  6. 6
    Ask for supportA registered dietitian or healthcare professional can help you make decisions suited to your circumstances.
